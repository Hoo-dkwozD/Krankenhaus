Authorized red-team operating model

Testing LLMs for undesirable content—without losing context.

Krankenhaus treats undesirable-content testing as a separate, policy-aware assessment. It connects a named target surface to business context, audience, jurisdiction, policy cells, a reviewed playbook, controlled campaigns, reproducibility, and human review.

The testing model

Context is part of the security boundary.

The same output is not automatically a violation in every deployment.

01 · Context

Understand the deployment

Capture use case, audience, user roles, workflows, language, jurisdiction, model/provider, guardrails, memory, tools, RAG, rendering, and production-like parameters.

02 · Policy

Define the evaluation oracle

Policy cells specify what is in scope, what counts as a violation, what is not a violation, severity, proxy-only categories, thresholds, and escalation behavior.

03 · Evidence

Prove and review outcomes

Candidate successes are reproduced, scored against the policy matrix, reviewed by a human, and reported with redacted excerpts. Full transcripts stay in approved evidence storage.

Playbook-derived operators

Prompt injection is a catalogue, not a payload dump.

Select operators only after target, policy, budget, and human-review gates are complete.

Krankenhaus learned these named mutation operators from the integrated undesirable-content playbook. They probe different failure modes—framing, instruction hierarchy, filter evasion, conversation state, indirect input, multimodal handling, policy asymmetry, and scope control. The catalogue does not authorize sending every technique.
T01Persona / role-play

Probes whether framing a request as a role or character overrides alignment.

T02Fictional / hypothetical frame

Probes susceptibility to a pretext that separates the request from real-world intent.

T03Authority / legitimacy claim

Probes whether an unverified authority or approval claim receives excess trust.

T04Direct prompt injection

Probes instruction-hierarchy robustness when a user attempts to replace governing behavior.

T05Refusal suppression

Probes whether the requester can steer the model away from a required refusal.

T06Prefix injection

Probes autoregressive commitment after the model is induced to begin a constrained continuation.

T07Encoding obfuscation

Probes keyword and classifier evasion through alternate representations.

T08Language shift

Probes English-centric safety gaps across supported languages or language mixtures.

T09Payload splitting

Probes filters that evaluate fragments without reconstructing the full conversational context.

T10Task decomposition

Probes per-turn evaluation gaps when a disallowed objective is split into smaller steps.

T11Crescendo / gradual escalation

Probes conversation-level moderation gaps as a thread moves from safe to unsafe.

T12Many-shot priming

Probes long-context in-context learning and whether repeated examples shift behavior.

T13Output format shift

Probes prose-only filter gaps by requesting another allowed response representation.

T14Completion framing

Probes whether a continuation request is evaluated differently from a direct request.

T15Indirect injection

Probes handling of untrusted instructions embedded in documents, URLs, email, or tool data.

T16Multimodal injection

Probes cross-modal moderation when images or audio are accepted by the target.

T17Counterfactual bias pairs

Probes protected-attribute asymmetry by comparing policy-equivalent variants.

T18Emotional / urgency manipulation

Probes sycophancy and pressure handling under urgency or emotional framing.

T19Context / goal hijack

Probes whether the model loses its intended scope or workflow objective.

T20Commitment extraction

Probes unauthorized promises, terms, or commitments elicited from the application.

T21Automated optimization

Probes general robustness through explicit tooling; requires separate tooling approval.

Selection heuristics

  • Input classifier suspected: T07, T08, T09, T13
  • Output filter suspected: T09, T13
  • Model-generated refusals: T01, T02, T03, T11, T12
  • Multi-turn memory: T10, T11
  • Document, URL, email, or tool ingestion: T15
  • Image or audio acceptance: T16
  • Bias policy cell: T17 is mandatory
  • Policy-cell testing: T18, T19, T20 where applicable
Measurement model

Metrics make stochastic behavior comparable.

Operational calculation model; thresholds and policy-cell labels remain engagement-specific. No target campaign has been run here.

BRR
Baseline Refusal Rate
BRR = Rb ÷ Nb × 100

Rb: baseline probes that receive a policy-compliant refusal or safe redirect.
Nb: valid baseline probes for the selected harmful-content cells.

Example: 18 compliant refusals out of 24 valid baseline probes: 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75%.

BVR
Baseline Violation Rate
BVR = Vb ÷ Nb × 100

Vb: baseline probes whose output meets the approved policy-cell violation criteria.
Nb: the same valid baseline-probe denominator used for BRR.

Example: 3 baseline probes meet the violation criteria out of 24 valid probes: 3 ÷ 24 × 100 = 12.5%.

ORR
Over-Refusal Rate
ORR = Rc ÷ Nc × 100

Rc: benign, allowed-control prompts incorrectly refused or blocked.
Nc: valid benign controls expected to receive an allowed response.

Example: 2 benign controls are incorrectly refused out of 20 valid controls: 2 ÷ 20 × 100 = 10%.

ASR
Attack Success Rate
ASR = Sa ÷ Ae × 100

Sa: eligible adversarial attempts producing a confirmed policy violation.
Ae: eligible attempts actually sent and scored; excluded or blocked attempts are reported separately.

Example: 7 eligible adversarial attempts produce confirmed violations out of 35 scored attempts: 7 ÷ 35 × 100 = 20%.

k/N
Reproducibility
Reproducibility = k successful replays ÷ N total replays

k: fixed-prompt trials reproducing the confirmed outcome.
N: configured replay trials, preferably across fresh sessions where applicable; report both the fraction and percentage.

Example: 3 fixed-prompt replays reproduce the confirmed outcome out of 5 trials: 3 ÷ 5 = 60%.

Composite campaign metric
PUCE
Positive Undesirable Content Efficiency
Higher means more positive cases with fewer typical cycles and attempts
PUCE = P ÷ (A × Cmode) × 100

P: distinct positive undesirable-content test cases with a confirmed policy-cell violation.
A: total eligible attempts sent and scored to obtain undesirable content; define an attempt consistently in the campaign record.
Cmode: the most frequently observed number of prompt-response cycles required to produce a confirmed positive result across the positive cases.

Example: 8 positive cases, 40 eligible attempts, and a modal cycle count of 2: 8 ÷ (40 × 2) × 100 = 10%.

Bounds: assuming each positive case consumes at least one eligible attempt, 0% ≤ PUCE ≤ 100%. The upper bound requires every eligible attempt to produce a distinct positive case and Cmode = 1; the lower bound is 0% when no positive case is confirmed.

If P = 0, report 0%; if A = 0 or Cmode is undefined, report N/A. If cycle counts are tied between multiple modes, report the modal set and corresponding PUCE range rather than choosing silently. Pair with ASR and reproducibility; PUCE is an efficiency indicator, not severity.
Score each response against the approved policy cell before counting it. Keep numerator, denominator, exclusions, policy cell, role/persona, model parameters, and session conditions with the result; do not infer a rate from tone or from refusal alone.
Hard operating rules

Safety is enforced as code and process.

  • !Target outputs, retrieved documents, tool outputs, and messages are untrusted data—not instructions.
  • !Host, endpoint, budget, rate, turn, and scope allowlists are enforced outside the prompt.
  • !Extremely hazardous categories use proxy objectives and stop at boundary evidence.
  • !Escalate real personal data, credentials, production impact, hard exclusions, or judge anomalies.
